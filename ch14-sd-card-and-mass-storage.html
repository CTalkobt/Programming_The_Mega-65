Programming the MEGA65 — Chapter 14: The SD Card and Mass Storage

Oct 4, 2026 · Updated Nov 13, 2026 · @Craig Taylor

Disk image: ch14.d81 holds this chapter's 2 complete programs. Each can also be downloaded from the links above its listing, and every listing has a Copy button.

Introduction

In West's book this chapter covered the C64's cassette tape. The MEGA65 has no cassette port. Its everyday mass storage is the SD card, which holds the system itself, the configuration, and the user's software and data, mostly in the form of disk images. This chapter covers the SD card as storage: how it is organised, how disk images on it are used, how BASIC and machine language reach its files directly, and how files get onto it. Chapter 15 then covers the disk commands that work on images and real drives alike.

SD Cards

The MEGA65 has two SD card slots: a full-size slot inside the case, reached from underneath, and a microSD slot at the rear. Only one is used at a time; a card in the rear slot takes over, and the internal card is ignored. The rear slot is the more convenient for moving a card between the MEGA65 and a PC (MEGA65 Book, p. 4-9).

The MEGA65 supports SDHC cards between 4GB and 32GB. Smaller cards and larger SDXC cards are not expected to work.

Partitions

An SD card must be prepared by the MEGA65's own SD Card Utility; a card formatted by a PC will not work, even if only its FAT32 partition is changed. The utility creates two partitions (MEGA65 Book, pp. 4-9, 4-10):

Partition Contents
Hidden Configuration settings and freeze states
FAT32 System files, disk images and other files, readable by a PC

A backup that copies only the files on the FAT32 partition misses the configuration and freeze states; a complete backup needs a tool that copies whole partitions.

The card starts with an ordinary PC partition table. Sector 0, the master boot record, holds four 16-byte entries from offset $1BE; in each, the byte at offset 4 is the partition's type, and the four bytes at offset 8 and the four at offset 12 are its first sector and its length in sectors, low byte first. The Hypervisor looks for two types: $0B or $0C, a FAT32 partition, and $41, the MEGA65 system partition (mega65-core, src/hyppo/dos.asm). The system partition is not a file system. Its first sector begins with the text MEGA65SYS00 and gives the position and size of two areas of fixed-size slots, one for frozen programs and one for installed services, each with a directory of 128-byte entries (mega65-core, src/hyppo/syspart.asm). A program has no reason to write there; the Freezer and the configuration utility look after it.

Preparing a card

Switch the MEGA65 off, hold ALT and switch it on to open the utility menu, then choose 2, the SD card utility. Choose the card to prepare, 0 for internal and 1 for the rear microSD, and confirm by typing DELETE EVERYTHING. The utility partitions the card and then copies the system files, MEGA65.ROM and the .M65 programs, from the core. Formatting cannot be undone, and the Book advises not erasing the internal card that came with the machine (MEGA65 Book, pp. 4-10, 4-11).

What is on the FAT32 partition

Files Purpose
MEGA65.ROM The ROM image loaded at power-on
.M65 files The Freezer and its utility programs
MEGA65.D81 The Intro Disk, mounted at start-up by default
Other .D81 and .D64 files Disk images of software and data
.PRG and other files Programs and data stored directly on the card

Files can be organised into subdirectories, which the Freezer's file browser and BASIC's CHDIR command understand.

FAT32 is the PC's file system, unchanged. The partition is divided into clusters of one or more 512-byte sectors; a table at its start, the FAT, has a 32-bit entry for each cluster that gives the number of the cluster that follows it in its file; and a directory is itself a chain of clusters holding 32-byte entries. An entry has the name in the first 11 bytes, eight characters and a three-character extension, an attribute byte at offset 11, the file's first cluster at offsets 20 and 26, two bytes each, and its length in bytes at offset 28. Longer names are kept in extra entries in front of the short one. The Hypervisor reads both kinds of name, and matches names without regard to case (mega65-core, src/hyppo/constants.asm, src/hyppo/dos.asm).

The system files belong with the core. Each core release includes a matching set in its sdcard-files folder, and the Book's upgrade steps copy them to the root of the card along with the new core (MEGA65 Book, Cores and Flashing). Skipping them leaves a new core running an old Freezer and old utilities, which can misbehave in ways that are hard to trace back to the update.

Disk Images

Most MEGA65 software is distributed as D81 files: images of 3.5" Commodore 1581 disks. A mounted image behaves exactly like a real disk in a drive, so every disk command in Chapter 15 works on it. The MEGA65 also mounts D64 images of 1541 disks.

Disk image formats

A disk image is a copy, block for block, of every sector of a floppy disk, held in one file. Each 256-byte sector of the disk is one block in the directory's counts:

Image Copy of File size Blocks Free when empty
D81 1581 3.5" disk: 80 tracks of 40 sectors 819,200 bytes 3,200 3,160
D64 1541 5.25" disk: 35 tracks of 17 to 21 sectors 174,848 bytes 683 664

In both, one track holds the directory and the BAM, the map of used blocks, which is why fewer blocks are free than the disk holds: track 40 of a D81 and track 18 of a D64 (MEGA65 ROM source, dos.src). The Hypervisor tells the two apart by their size when it mounts them (mega65-core, src/hyppo/dos.asm).

D81 is the MEGA65's own format and the one to use for new work. It holds almost five times as much as a D64, and can be formatted on the MEGA65 with HEADER, or created in the Freezer.

D64 support is for running and reading existing 1541 software. The ROM gained it in 920403, after a first attempt in 920381 was withdrawn, and later ROMs fixed several problems with it (MEGA65 ROM change log). The DOS formats only 1581 disks and D81 images, so new D64 images have to be made on a PC (MEGA65 ROM source, dos.src).

The Hypervisor also recognises the sizes of D71 images (1571 disks) and D65 images, but the DOS in the ROM handles only D81 and D64 (MEGA65 ROM source, dos.src). A D65 is the image of a high-density "MEGA disk": 85 tracks of 64 sectors on each of two sides, 512 bytes a sector, which comes to 5,570,560 bytes (mega65-core, src/hyppo/dos.asm, sdcardio.vhdl).

Inside a D81 image

The blocks are stored in order, track 1 first and 40 blocks to a track, so block S of track T starts at this offset in the file:

offset = ((T - 1) * 40 + S) * 256

Tracks are numbered from 1 and sectors from 0, as the DOS numbers them. Track 40 holds the system blocks, which are those of a 1581 disk (Commodore 1581 Disk Drive User's Guide):

Track, sector File offset Contents
40, 0 $61800 Header: the track and sector of the first directory block (40, 3) in bytes 0 and 1, the disk name in bytes 4 to 19, padded with $A0, the ID in bytes 22 and 23 and the DOS type in bytes 25 and 26, 3D from a 1581 and 1D from the MEGA65's DOS
40, 1 $61900 BAM for tracks 1 to 40: from byte 16, six bytes for each track, the number of free blocks and then 40 bits, one for each sector, set while the sector is free
40, 2 $61A00 BAM for tracks 41 to 80, in the same form
40, 3 to 40, 39 $61B00 The directory: eight 32-byte entries in each block, 296 in all

Every block of a file, and of the directory, begins with the track and sector of the next block; in the last block the track is 0 and the second byte is the number of the last byte used. Chapter 15 gives the layout of a directory entry and the commands that read and write single blocks.

Managed drives and unit numbers

The MEGA65 provides two managed drives alongside anything on the IEC port (MEGA65 Book, pp. 6-3, 6-4):

Managed drive Can hold Unit number
First A D81 or D64 image, or the internal 3.5" drive 8 or 10
Second A D81 or D64 image 9 or 11

Commands addressed to these units are handled by the MEGA65; any other unit number goes to the IEC port. Chapter 15 covers the drives themselves, the internal 3.5" drive and those on the IEC port.

Mounting

In the Freezer, press 0 or 1 to choose a managed drive, pick an image from the file browser, and press 8 or 9 to change its unit number. From BASIC, MOUNT does the same, using the current directory on the card:

MOUNT "MEGA65.D81"
MOUNT "GAMES.D81", U9

The Freezer can also create a new, formatted D81 image: choose the first managed drive and select NEW D81 DD IMAGE at the top of the file list (MEGA65 Book, pp. 6-5, 6-6).

Changing disks from a program

MOUNT works in a program as well as in direct mode, so software that fills more than one image can change disks itself, without sending the user to the Freezer (MEGA65 Book, p. B-171):

500 MOUNT "PART2.D81"        : REM PUT THE SECOND IMAGE IN UNIT 8
510 DOPEN#2, "MAP"
520 IF DS THEN PRINT DS$ : STOP

MOUNT's U argument names the managed drive, always as U8 or U9, even when SET DISK or the Freezer has given the drive another unit number. Several more commands help a program manage its drives:

  • MOUNT OFF (ROM 920414) empties both managed drives, like the Freezer's "no disk" setting, while MOUNT with no file name gives unit 8 back to the internal drive. From ROM 920415 both take a U argument to act on one drive only, as in MOUNT OFF U9.
  • Remembered mounts. MOUNT remembers the images it mounted and mounts them again after the reset button is pressed. From ROM 920414, MOUNT with no file name, or MOUNT OFF, makes it forget them, so the configured default image is mounted after the next reset. Images mounted from the Freezer are not remembered in this way (MEGA65 Book, p. B-171).
  • SET DISK old TO new changes a drive's unit number until the next reset, as the Freezer's 8 and 9 keys do. It arrived in ROM 920380.
  • RDISK(1) returns the unit number of the most recent disk operation (ROM 920414). At the start of a program, that is the unit the program was loaded from, so the program can load its data files from the same drive, whichever one the user chose:
10 DU = RDISK(1)                 : REM THE UNIT THIS PROGRAM CAME FROM
20 DOPEN#2, "SETTINGS", U(DU)

The ROM versions are from the MEGA65 ROM change log. ROM 920413, in release 0.97, has SET DISK but not MOUNT OFF or RDISK; Chapter 11 shows how a program can check the version it is running on.

The SD card as unit 12

A few BASIC commands can reach files on the card directly, outside any disk image, by using unit 12 (MEGA65 Book, pp. 6-10, 6-11):

DIR U12, P                  : REM LIST THE CARD, A PAGE AT A TIME
CHDIR "DEMOS", U12          : REM CHANGE THE CURRENT DIRECTORY
CHDIR "..", U12             : REM BACK TO THE PARENT
DLOAD "MYPROGRAM.PRG", U12  : REM NOTE THE .PRG SUFFIX

These are typed in direct mode. In a program, DLOAD replaces the program that is running, so it can only be the last thing a program does.

Unit 12 is not a full disk drive. Directory listing, loading with DLOAD or LOAD, TYPE (from ROM 920417) and CHDIR work on it, and from ROM 920410 DIR also takes a pattern, as in DIR "M*", U12; DSAVE refuses unit 12, so a program cannot save to the card this way, and the other file commands, BLOAD and DOPEN among them, have no unit 12 form: given U12 they look for a drive numbered 12 on the IEC port. Data files therefore belong in a disk image, or are read through the Hypervisor, as described below (MEGA65 ROM source, b65.src; MEGA65 ROM change log). MOUNT does not take U12, because its U argument chooses the unit to mount the image on, but it does use the directory set by CHDIR. Support for subdirectories is still developing; for example, changing the current directory unmounts an image that was mounted from a subdirectory (MEGA65 Book, p. 6-6). The Freezer has a similar limit. It records only the file name of each mounted image, not the directory it came from, and it warns that starting one of its tools, which changes to the root directory, breaks mounts from subdirectories (mega65-freezemenu, freezer.c). Keeping disk images in the root directory, or mounting them with MOUNT from a program, avoids the problem.

Booting from an image

The configuration utility chooses an image to mount at power-on, and a program named AUTOBOOT.C65 on that image runs automatically. Booting Disks in Chapter 15 covers this and the other ways to start software from a disk.

SD Card Files from Machine Language

The KERNAL reaches disk images through the usual OPEN, LOAD and SAVE calls. For files on the card itself, machine language uses the Hypervisor's file services, which work even when a program has switched the KERNAL out. Every service is called the same way: put its number in A, write A to the trap register $D640, and follow the write with CLV. On return, carry set means success; carry clear means failure, and service $38 then returns the error code in A. The trap register exists only in the MEGA65 I/O personality (MEGA65 Book, pp. N-4, N-5). These are the services for files and images (mega65-core, src/hyppo/dos.asm):

Service A Use
setname $2E Set the file name: a page-aligned ASCII name ending in a zero byte, page in Y
findfile $34 Find the named file in the current directory; needed before openfile
findfirst, findnext $30, $32 Search the current directory
openfile, readfile, closefile $18, $1A, $20 Read a file a sector at a time. openfile returns a file descriptor in A, which closefile takes in X; readfile returns the number of bytes read in X (low) and Y (high), 0 at the end of the file
loadfile $36 Load the whole file to chip RAM
loadfile_attic $3E Load the whole file to attic RAM
geterrorcode $38 Return the error code of the last failed service in A
chdir, cdrootdir $0C, $3C Change directory
attach $4A Mount or unmount a disk image: X bit 0 selects the drive, bit 7 unmounts. The older d81attach0 and d81attach1 ($40, $46) still work but are deprecated from Hyppo 1.3
d81write_en $44 Allow writing to the attached image

Chapter 8 gives a complete loading example and the rules for setname, and Chapter 11 lists every service and which ones the current Hypervisor implements. loadfile_attic can load a large file, such as a game's levels or music, straight into attic RAM at start-up.

The Hypervisor can write as well as read, but only just: writefile ($1C) writes a sector to a file that already exists, and mkfile ($1E), which creates one, is only partly implemented. Chapter 11 gives their limits. A program that must save data should keep it in a disk image and use the KERNAL.

Reading a file a sector at a time

readfile does not copy anything into the program's memory. It reads the next 512-byte sector of the file into the SD card's sector buffer, which a program reaches at $FFD6E00-$FFD6FFF, and moves on to the following sector (mega65-core, src/hyppo/dos.asm, iomapper.vhdl). This routine opens a file, reads its first sector, takes the first two bytes from the buffer and closes the file:

buf     = $fb             ; four bytes: pointer to $FFD6E00

readf   ldy #>fname       ; page of the name
        lda #$2e          ; setname
        sta $d640
        clv
        bcc fail
        lda #$34          ; findfile
        sta $d640
        clv
        bcc fail
        lda #$18          ; openfile
        sta $d640
        clv
        bcc fail
        sta fd            ; A = file descriptor
        lda #$1a          ; readfile: one sector into the buffer
        sta $d640
        clv
        stx count         ; X/Y = number of bytes read,
        sty count+1       ; 0 at the end of the file
        lda #$00          ; point at the buffer, $FFD6E00
        sta buf
        lda #$6e
        sta buf+1
        lda #$fd
        sta buf+2
        lda #$0f
        sta buf+3
        ldz #0
        lda [buf],z       ; first byte of the file
        sta first
        inz
        lda [buf],z
        sta first+1
        ldz #0            ; leave Z at 0, for code that uses STZ
        ldx fd
        lda #$20          ; closefile
        sta $d640
        clv
        sec               ; success
        rts
fail    lda #$38          ; geterrorcode: A = the reason
        sta $d640
        clv
        clc
        rts

fd      .byte 0
count   .word 0
first   .byte 0, 0

        .enc "ascii"      ; names are ASCII, not PETSCII
        .cdef " ~", 32
        .align 256        ; and must start a page
fname   .text "FREEZER.M65", 0

Call readfile again for each further sector, until it returns a count of 0. The two .enc and .cdef lines are 64tass's way of storing text unconverted; without them, and with the assembler told that its source is ASCII, the name is stored as PETSCII and the Hypervisor reports error $88, file not found. Run in Xemu with ROM 920413, the routine returns a count of $0200 and the bytes $01, $08, the load address at the front of that file.

Mounting an image from machine language

attach needs only the image's name. The image must be in the current directory of the card and in one piece, as explained under Keeping disk images in one piece:

mount   ldy #>iname       ; page of the image's name
        lda #$2e          ; setname
        sta $d640
        clv
        bcc mfail
        ldx #$01          ; X bit 0: drive 0 or 1; bit 7 clear: attach
        lda #$4a          ; attach
        sta $d640
        clv
mfail   rts               ; carry set: mounted

        .align 256
iname   .text "MEGA65.D81", 0   ; ASCII, as above

This mounts the image on the second managed drive; X = 0 chooses the first, and X = $80 or $81 unmounts one. The service needs Hyppo 1.3; on an older Hypervisor use d81attach0 or d81attach1 instead. It does only the Hypervisor's part of the job. BASIC's MOUNT also remembers the image for the next reset and tells the DOS about the change, so a program that keeps the KERNAL running is better served by a short BASIC loader that gives MOUNT.

Reading sectors of the card

Beneath the Hypervisor's file services is the SD card controller, which reads and writes whole 512-byte sectors of the card, whatever partition they belong to (mega65-core, sdcardio.vhdl):

Register Use
$D680 Write a command; read the status. Commands: $02 read the sector, $03 write it, $C0 and $C1 choose the internal or the external card. Status: bits 0 and 1 are set while the controller or the card is busy, bit 4 is set for an SDHC card, and bits 5 and 6 report an error
$D681-$D684 The sector number, low byte first
$D689 bit 7 Chooses what a program sees at $FFD6E00, and at $DE00 when the buffer is mapped there: 1 for the SD card's sector buffer, 0 for the floppy controller's
$FFD6E00-$FFD6FFF The sector buffer

To read a sector, set bit 7 of $D689, write the sector number, write $02 to $D680, wait for the two busy bits to clear, and take the data from the buffer. Clear bit 7 of $D689 afterwards: it is clear while BASIC runs, and the ROM's DOS does not set it for itself. This program reads sector 0 and prints the partition table described at the start of the chapter:

10 POKE $D689, PEEK($D689) OR $80   : REM SHOW THE CARD'S BUFFER
20 POKE $D681, 0, 0, 0, 0           : REM SECTOR 0
30 POKE $D680, 2                    : REM READ IT
40 DO WHILE PEEK($D680) AND 3 : LOOP
50 B = $FFD6E00
60 FOR P = 0 TO 3 : E = B + $1BE + 16 * P
70 T = PEEK(E + 4) : IF T = 0 THEN 110
80 S = PEEK(E + 8) + 256 * PEEK(E + 9) + 65536 * PEEK(E + 10) + 16777216 * PEEK(E + 11)
90 N = PEEK(E + 12) + 256 * PEEK(E + 13) + 65536 * PEEK(E + 14) + 16777216 * PEEK(E + 15)
100 PRINT "PARTITION"; P + 1; "TYPE $"; RIGHT$(HEX$(T), 2); " FROM SECTOR"; S; "FOR"; N
110 NEXT
120 POKE $D689, PEEK($D689) AND $7F  : REM GIVE THE BUFFER BACK

In Xemu it prints a FAT32 partition, type $0C, from sector 2048, and a system partition, type $41, after it. Writing is deliberately harder. The controller ignores command $03 unless $57 has been written to $D680 just before it, and for sector 0 it needs $4D instead (mega65-core, sdcardio.vhdl). A wrong sector number destroys files or the whole card, so leave writing to the Hypervisor and the Freezer unless the program is a disk utility and has been tested on a card that does not matter.

Getting Files onto the Card

There are three ways to move files between a PC and the MEGA65's SD card:

  • Move the card. Switch off, take the microSD card out of the rear slot, copy files on a PC, and put it back. Simple, but slow for frequent changes.
  • Ethernet. With the MEGA65 on a network, the M65Connect application and the mega65_ftp command-line tool transfer files to and from the active card while the card stays in place. This is the fastest method for development (MEGA65 Book, Chapter 7).
  • Download. The MEGA65 Filehost website, files.mega65.org, hosts a library of community software as D81 images, which can be copied to the card by either method.

Cross-development tools, covered in the Book's development chapters, can also load and run a program in memory over USB serial without writing it to the card at all.

Keeping disk images in one piece

The Hypervisor mounts a disk image only if the image is stored in one contiguous run of clusters on the card. It checks this as it attaches the image and otherwise fails with error $8B, as Chapter 11 lists (mega65-core, src/hyppo/dos.asm). A PC's file system is free to split a file into pieces, so a disk image copied onto a card that already holds other files, or overwritten in place, may become fragmented and refuse to mount.

Three habits avoid it:

  • Transfer files with mega65_ftp, which places each file in contiguous free clusters (mega65-tools, mega65_ftp.c).
  • When using a PC, copy everything onto a freshly formatted card in one go, so each file is written in one piece.
  • Defragment the card's FAT32 partition on the PC after copying.

The Freezer and the Hypervisor create new images as one contiguous block, so images made on the MEGA65 itself are safe.

Summary

  • Cards. SDHC, 4 to 32GB, in the internal or rear slot; the rear card takes precedence. Cards must be prepared with the MEGA65's SD Card Utility, which creates a hidden configuration partition and a FAT32 partition.
  • Disk images. D81 and D64 images are mounted from the Freezer or with MOUNT to two managed drives, units 8 or 10 and 9 or 11. The Freezer can create new images. D81 holds 3,160 free blocks, D64 664; the DOS cannot format D64 images.
  • Changing disks. Programs can call MOUNT and MOUNT OFF, renumber drives with SET DISK, and find their own unit with RDISK(1).
  • Unit 12. DIR, DLOAD, LOAD, TYPE and CHDIR can reach files on the card directly; DSAVE cannot save to it.
  • Machine language. Hypervisor services, called through $D640, load, read and search card files, and mount images, independent of the KERNAL. The SD controller at $D680 reads single sectors into the buffer at $FFD6E00.
  • Layout. The card has a PC partition table, a FAT32 partition and a system partition of type $41. A D81 keeps its header, BAM and directory on track 40.
  • Transfer. Move the card, or use M65Connect or mega65_ftp over Ethernet.
